1. If you think the county valued your property incorrectly, look up your [county assessor](https://comptroller.tn.gov/office-functions/pa/tax-resources/assessment-information-for-each-county/assessors-and-trustees.html). Ask how to file with the county board of equalization and when it hears appeals. An informal discussion with the assessor does not count as filing the county appeal. Most owners need that formal step to preserve a state appeal. [County board instructions](https://www.comptroller.tn.gov/boards/state-board-of-equalization/sboe-resources/county-board-of-equalization.html) · [Assessment appeal process](https://comptroller.tn.gov/boards/state-board-of-equalization/value-appeals.html)
2. If you disagree with the county board’s decision, keep the notice. Check the [state board’s deadline](https://comptroller.tn.gov/boards/state-board-of-equalization/value-appeals.html), then use **File A Value Appeal**. Have your property information ready. The [filing portal](https://comptroller.tn.gov/boards/state-board-of-equalization/value-appeals/redirect-file-a-value-appeal.html) explains the fee, exemptions, and what to do about taxes due while the appeal is pending.
3. Once the state board accepts the filing, follow its instructions for scheduling a hearing. Use **SBOE Public Portal** on the [value appeals page](https://comptroller.tn.gov/boards/state-board-of-equalization/value-appeals.html) to check the status of an existing appeal.

If you need the parcel ID for your appeal, look up the property in the map for its county. [Find your county’s property map](find-a-property-map.md)
