A Tennessee law can start with two bill numbers. Sponsors often file the same proposal in the House and Senate. The House bill (HB) and Senate bill (SB) are called companions. Keep both numbers when you [look up a proposal](track-a-bill.md). [How a bill becomes a law](https://wapp.capitol.tn.gov/apps/Information/About/BillToLaw)

1. **Filing and referral.** A legislator files a bill. The clerk gives it a number and the chamber refers it to a committee that handles the subject. An entry such as *passed on first consideration* is an early procedural step. [Legislative process](https://wapp.capitol.tn.gov/apps/Information/About/BillToLaw)
2. **Committee review.** The sponsor explains the proposal. A committee can recommend passage, recommend changes, or leave the bill in committee. A recommendation sends the proposal forward for further consideration. You can [find the meeting and its agenda](find-legislative-hearings.md). [Committee and calendar stages](https://wapp.capitol.tn.gov/apps/Information/About/BillToLaw)
3. **House and Senate votes.** Each chamber considers the bill. A chamber can amend the text before voting. If the second chamber changes language the first one passed, the first can agree to the change or the chambers can work through the difference. A conference committee can propose a compromise. Both chambers must agree on the same text. [Floor votes and companion bills](https://wapp.capitol.tn.gov/apps/Information/About/BillToLaw)
4. **Final text and governor’s action.** An *engrossed* bill includes the amendments a chamber passed. The *enrolled* bill contains the text both chambers passed and goes to the governor after the speakers sign it. The governor can sign it, veto it, or allow it to become law without a signature. The legislature can override a veto. [Legislative glossary](https://wapp.capitol.tn.gov/apps/Information/About/Glossary) · [Governor’s action](https://wapp.capitol.tn.gov/apps/Information/About/BillToLaw)
5. **Publication and effective date.** The Secretary of State assigns a public chapter number to a general bill that becomes law. Find that entry in the bill history, open the enacted text, and read the effective-date clause for the provision you are checking. [Bill history FAQ](https://wapp.capitol.tn.gov/apps/Information/Help/Index) · [How to read a public chapter](read-a-public-chapter.md) · [Public chapter reports](https://wapp.capitol.tn.gov/apps/WebPublications)

If the proposal people are discussing sounds different from the description on the bill page, [compare the introduced bill with its amendments](read-a-caption-bill.md). A proposal to change the state Constitution takes a different route through two elected General Assemblies and a statewide vote. [Track a constitutional amendment](track-a-constitutional-amendment.md)
