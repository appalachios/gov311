**If the notice says General Sessions**, start with the county printed on it and check whether it names a civil or criminal division. Look for that county in the General Sessions clerk directory. Some counties have a separate clerk established by private act. In most counties without one, the Circuit Court Clerk handles General Sessions. If your county is missing from the shorter General Sessions list, use the Circuit Court Clerk directory. Give the clerk the case number and division. Ask for the next hearing date, courtroom or docket, or identify the specific order you want to copy. [General Sessions clerks](https://www.ctas.tennessee.edu/general-sessions-court-clerks) · [Circuit Court clerks](https://www.ctas.tennessee.edu/circuit-court-clerks) · [Clerk assignments under Tennessee Code § 16-15-301](https://www.ctas.tennessee.edu/eli/clerks-court)

Davidson County publishes separate General Sessions civil and criminal docket instructions. Choose the division shown on the papers. If the online docket does not show your case, call that division to confirm the hearing. [Davidson County General Sessions case information](https://gscourt.nashville.gov/court-case-information/)

Read the name of the court on your paperwork. For a case in the Tennessee Supreme Court, Court of Appeals, or Court of Criminal Appeals, use the Administrative Office of the Courts’ [Appellate Case Search](https://pch.tncourts.gov/). You can search by case number, the case name as it appears on the papers, a party’s name, or a business name.

If the papers name a trial court, look up the county in the [court clerk directory](https://www.ctas.tennessee.edu/clerks-of-court). Give the clerk the court name and case number and ask for the docket or record you need. This route also covers an appeal being heard in a trial court. For example, Davidson County's Circuit Court hears civil appeals from General Sessions Court.
