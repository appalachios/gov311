Open the **Public Chapter** link on the bill's page to read the law as enacted. For example, the history of Senate Bill 259 in the 113th General Assembly links to Public Chapter 336 of 2023, a law about driver licenses. [SB 259 bill history](https://wapp.capitol.tn.gov/apps/BillInfo/Default?BillNumber=SB0259&ga=113) · [Public Chapter 336](https://publications.tnsosfiles.com/acts/113/pub/pc0336.pdf)

1. **Find the part of the code that changed.** A section number in the public chapter tells you which instruction you are reading. The longer number inside that instruction tells you where to find the law in the Tennessee Code Annotated. Section 4 of this chapter says to delete and replace subdivision 55-12-115(b)(2). The replacement refers to (b)(1), so read both subdivisions together. Section 9 makes a different kind of change. It adds subsection (f) to 55-50-303. [Public Chapter 336, sections 4 and 9](https://publications.tnsosfiles.com/acts/113/pub/pc0336.pdf)
2. **Read the closing sections.** Some instructions are *uncodified*. They govern the act without becoming numbered sections of the code. Section 11 gives one effective date for making agency rules and a later one for other purposes. It also tells you which violations the change covers. The date the governor signed the bill alone cannot tell you whether the change applies to an earlier event. [Public Chapter 336, section 11](https://publications.tnsosfiles.com/acts/113/pub/pc0336.pdf#page=5) · [General Assembly drafting guide, chapter 5](https://capitol.tn.gov/Archives/Joint/staff/legal/Drafting%20Guide%202025.pdf)
3. **Check the law in effect for your question.** Open **Tennessee Code** from the General Assembly's publications page and look up the cited code section. Check how current the edition is. If the legislature has met since that update, check later public chapters for changes to the same section and read their effective dates. [Tennessee Code and public chapter reports](https://wapp.capitol.tn.gov/apps/WebPublications)
