A Tennessee bill’s *caption* is the title near the top of the filed bill. It names the subject and often the parts of state law the bill would change. The caption stays fixed after filing. An amendment can replace the entire proposal underneath it, provided the change fits the subject and the code provisions named in the caption. This is why a bill about “education” can change substantially while keeping its title. [Legislative Drafting Guide, chapters 5(b) and 6(d)](https://capitol.tn.gov/Archives/Joint/staff/legal/Drafting%20Guide%202025.pdf)

If the history calls a proposal a *caption bill*, look for the amendment that supplies the proposal legislators will consider. For example, in the 114th General Assembly, House Rule 47 provided for holding a designated caption bill at the clerk’s desk pending an amendment. If the amendment has yet to appear, the filed text alone cannot tell you what the sponsor plans to propose. [House Rule 47](https://capitol.tn.gov/Archives/House/114GA/publications/114th%20Permanent%20Rules%20of%20Order.pdf#page=11)

## How to compare versions

1. Find the House and Senate bill numbers for the session. Open the filed bill and read the first numbered section below the enacting clause, the line beginning “Be it enacted.” A description marked **as introduced** refers to this version. [Find and track a bill](track-a-bill.md)
2. Open **Amendments**. Read the instruction above the new text. An amendment that adds a section leaves other sections in place. One that deletes everything after the enacting clause and substitutes new sections replaces the original proposal. [Legislative Drafting Guide, chapter 6](https://capitol.tn.gov/Archives/Joint/staff/legal/Drafting%20Guide%202025.pdf)
3. Match each amendment number to **Bill History**. Look for adoption, withdrawal, and passage in each chamber. When one chamber substitutes the other chamber’s bill, follow the number it actually passed. The **Summary** can help you understand the changes. The **Fiscal Note** estimates the financial effect of the bill it identifies. If there is a fiscal memorandum, match the amendment it analyzes to the amendment the chamber adopted. [Example bill history and fiscal documents](https://wapp.capitol.tn.gov/apps/BillInfo/Default?BillNumber=HB2149&ga=114) · [How a bill becomes law](legislative-process.md)

## How a school bill changed in 2026

House Bill 2149, paired with Senate Bill 2433, had a caption covering education under Title 49. In the filed bill, Section 1 proposed deleting the code section for a pilot program on parent-teacher engagement. On February 5, the House recorded that it was holding the caption bill for an amendment. [Filed HB 2149](https://capitol.tn.gov/Bills/114/Bill/HB2149.pdf) · [Law establishing the pilot](https://wapp.capitol.tn.gov/apps/BillInfo/Default?BillNumber=HB2060&GA=110) · [Bill history](https://wapp.capitol.tn.gov/apps/BillInfo/Default?BillNumber=HB2149&ga=114)

House amendment **HA0871** replaced the proposed repeal. Its subject was what a school district or public charter school could do if a student withdrew during a disciplinary investigation and later reenrolled. The February fiscal note assessed the bill as filed. The March fiscal memorandum assessed this new proposal. Its draft number, **014594**, also appears at the foot of the amendment. [House amendment](https://capitol.tn.gov/Bills/114/Amend/HA0871.pdf) · [Original fiscal note](https://capitol.tn.gov/Bills/114/Fiscal/HB2149.pdf) · [Fiscal memorandum on the amendment](https://capitol.tn.gov/Bills/114/Fiscal/FM2285.pdf)

On March 30 the House adopted HA0871 and passed the amended bill. On April 2 the Senate substituted HB 2149 for its companion, withdrew **SA0802**, and passed the House bill. Both amendment PDFs appear on the bill page. The votes explain why the House amendment is the one to follow. [Senate amendment](https://capitol.tn.gov/Bills/114/Amend/SA0802.pdf) · [House and Senate actions](https://wapp.capitol.tn.gov/apps/BillInfo/Default?BillNumber=HB2149&ga=114)

The governor signed the bill on April 16. Open **Public Chapter 760** and compare its first section with HA0871. The enacted text contains the student investigation and reenrollment provisions. The proposed repeal of the parent-teacher pilot dropped out. [Public Chapter 760](https://publications.tnsosfiles.com/acts/114/pub/pc0760.pdf) · [General Assembly public chapter report](https://capitol.tn.gov/Archives/Joint/publications/PublicChapters/2026/PublicChapters_202601.pdf)
