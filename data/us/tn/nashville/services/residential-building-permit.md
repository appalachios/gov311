## Who needs this

Metro decides which projects need a building permit. Gov311 does not restate that list. Read it on Metro's pages.

- [Residential building permits](https://www.nashville.gov/departments/codes/construction-and-permits/residential-building-permits) lists the kinds of work that need a permit and the normal maintenance that does not.
- [Building permit required list (PDF)](https://www.nashville.gov/sites/default/files/2022-09/Building-Permit-Required.pdf) is Metro's project-by-project list.

If you are unsure, call the Zoning Help Desk at 615-862-6510 before you start work.

## Before you start

Gather these documents.

- The [Residential Building Permit Application](https://www.nashville.gov/sites/default/files/2024-04/Codes-Residential-Building-Permit-Application.pdf).
- A site plan for an addition or accessory structure. A site plan is a drawing of your lot that shows the distance from the project to the property lines, other structures, and easements. Metro notes that a mortgage loan survey works well as a site plan.
- A floor plan for an addition or renovation.
- Elevation drawings (side views) if the project adds height or is a new accessory structure.
- Construction plans, if Metro requires them for a home of your size. The residential permit page states the size.

Look up your property's zoning on the [Davidson County Parcel Viewer](https://maps.nashville.gov/ParcelViewer/). The base zoning and any overlays appear under the zoning tab.

## Steps for homeowners

A property owner who lives in, or plans to live in, a single-family home can get a permit to build or repair it without hiring a contractor. Metro calls this a self permit. The owner takes on all responsibility for the work.

1. Complete the permit application.
2. Complete the Affidavit of Exemption and the Self Permit Affidavit, and have both notarized. Metro requires the notarization before you submit them.
3. Email the application and your documents to zoninghelpdesk@nashville.gov. A zoning examiner is assigned to review the application.
4. Wait for approvals. Most applications also need approval from other departments, such as Water and Sewer, Health, Planning, Public Works, or Historical Zoning.
5. Pay the fees. Metro issues the permit with an inspection checklist that lists the inspections your project needs.
6. Schedule each inspection on the checklist as the work reaches that stage.
7. After the final inspection, contact the Use and Occupancy Division for the final letter.

Metro's pages disagree about how a homeowner submits the application. The residential permit page says to email the Zoning Help Desk. The ePermits page says homeowners must come to the office. Call 615-862-6510 to confirm before you submit.

A self permit cannot be used for a detached accessory dwelling unit (DADU), a second small home on the same lot.

## Steps for contractors

Registered, licensed contractors can apply online through [ePermits](https://epermits.nashville.gov/#/). To get a username and password, email permitissuance@nashville.gov. Registered ePermits users can also request and schedule inspections online.

## Trade permits

Electrical, plumbing, gas and mechanical, and low-voltage work each need a separate trade permit. A licensed subcontractor gets the trade permit after the building permit is issued. Each trade permit has its own fee.

## Cost

Metro calculates the building permit fee from the value of the project, then adds a zoning examination fee and a technology fee. Trade permits and re-inspections have their own fees.

Gov311 does not restate the amounts, because they change. Read them in Metro's [Codes fee schedule](https://www.nashville.gov/sites/default/files/2022-12/Codes-Fees-Schedule-221215.pdf). The schedule that Metro's permit process page links to is dated December 2022, so ask the Codes Department at 615-862-6500 whether it is still current.

## Common mistakes

- Starting work before the permit is issued.
- Submitting self-permit affidavits that are not notarized.
- Forgetting the trade permits for electrical, plumbing, and mechanical work.

## Where to get help

Call the Zoning Help Desk at 615-862-6510, or see Metro's [Building Permits Central](https://www.nashville.gov/departments/codes/construction-and-permits/building-permits-central) page.
