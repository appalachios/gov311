The Brighton charter (Private Acts of 1977, chapter 98, as amended) sets no general number of ordinance readings. One reading can suffice under Tennessee law when the charter is silent and no adopted local rule or subject-specific law requires more. Check the rule for the proposal before treating its first vote as final. [Compiled charter](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/sites/default/files/private/charters/Brighton_cht.pdf#page=9) · [Tennessee ordinance procedure](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/reference/procedures-adopting-ordinances) · [Tennessee Supreme Court, *Balsinger*, 435 S.W.2d 803, 805](https://law.justia.com/cases/tennessee/supreme-court/1968/435-s-w-2d-803-2.html)

Brighton’s signed beer-sales ordinance records a first reading in October 2026 and a second on November 10. The town’s index labels it 20231013, but the signed ordinance is **20261013**. Use that number when requesting the meeting record. To find out whether another measure needs two readings, ask the town recorder for the board rule or code section that governs adoption. Charter § 18 gives the mayor a tie-breaking vote and requires a unanimous board vote to override a veto. [Town ordinance index](https://www.townofbrighton.com/government/ordinances.php) · [Signed Ordinance 20261013](https://webgen1files1.revize.com/brightontn/Documents/Government/Ordinances/Ordinance%2020261013%20Sale%20of%20Beer%20Hours%20Amended.pdf)

To follow a proposal in Brighton, get the dated meeting notice, agenda, proposed text and attachments. The municipality reports its regular meeting schedule as “2nd Tuesday at 7:00 PM at Town Hall.” Confirm the date and location before attending. [Meeting schedule and municipal officials](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/directories/cities/brighton) · [Municipal meeting agendas](https://www.townofbrighton.com/government/agenda_and_minutes.php)

After the meeting, use the minutes to identify the motion that actually passed. Ask the recorder or clerk for the adopted ordinance, any amendments and the effective date. A proposal can change during consideration, and a recent ordinance may still be waiting to appear in the code. [Tennessee agenda and minutes requirements](https://comptroller.tn.gov/office-functions/open-records-counsel/open-meetings/frequently-asked-questions/tennessee-open-meetings-act-faqs.html)
