1. Call **423-496-3546** with the property address. Ducktown's building inspector handles city building permits with the city manager. Outside municipal limits, Polk County directs applicants to its property assessor in Benton. [City zoning ordinance, § 13.02](https://www.sedev.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/08.01.24-Ducktown-Zoning-Ordinance.pdf) · [Polk County permit instructions](https://polkcountytn.gov/property-assessor/)
2. Describe what you want to build, remove, repair or use differently. Ask what plans and permits you need before work begins. Section 13.03 allows the inspector to approve certain minor repairs without a permit and distinguishes work that needs a permit from work that carries a fee. [Ducktown zoning ordinance, § 13.03](https://www.sedev.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/08.01.24-Ducktown-Zoning-Ordinance.pdf)
3. Ask what final inspection and certificate of occupancy the city requires before you move in or open. The county assessor's statement that the county does not require an occupancy certificate applies outside the cities. Ducktown has its own occupancy rule in § 13.05. [City occupancy rule](https://www.sedev.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/08.01.24-Ducktown-Zoning-Ordinance.pdf) · [County guidance](https://polkcountytn.gov/property-assessor/)

**City Hall is at 1646 Highway 68 North.** The county assessor's permit page still lists 340 Main Street. The federal station locator identifies that site's building as the former City Hall. The county's city directory and the University of Tennessee list the Highway 68 location for city offices. Call before taking plans in. [County city directory](https://polkcountytn.gov/city-offices/) · [Municipal office directory](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/directories/cities/ducktown) · [Federal station record for 340 Main](https://afdc.energy.gov/stations#/station/197819)
