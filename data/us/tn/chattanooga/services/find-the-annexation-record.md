## Tiftonia and Wauhatchee–Williams Island

On February 2, 1972, Chattanooga adopted Ordinance 6397 for Tiftonia (Area 4) and Ordinance 6393 for Wauhatchee–Williams Island (Area 12). To see where Area 12 met Area 4, open page 35 of the Tennessee Court of Appeals’ opinion in *Little v. City of Chattanooga*. It reproduces the map attached to Ordinance 6393. Page 34 shows the earlier map attached to the service plan, Resolution 9166. The line moved between the two maps. The court confirms on page 36 that the ordinance map shows the correct Area 12 boundary. [Court opinion and both maps, pages 34–36](https://cases.justia.com/tennessee/court-of-appeals/2022-e2020-01414-coa-r3-cv.pdf#page=34)

## Elder Mountain

A separate case, *Rich v. City of Chattanooga*, traces annexations in 1972 and 1994 involving lots on Cumberland Road. In 2011 the city proposed removing 36 residential lots from the city under Ordinance 12525. A court voided the August 2012 vote. In the replacement election on March 5, 2013, **25 voted to keep the land in the city and 24 voted to remove it**. Ordinance 12525 did not shrink the city limits. [Court opinion, pages 2–5](https://cases.justia.com/tennessee/court-of-appeals/2014-e2013-00190-coa-r3-cv.pdf#page=2) · [Official ballot](https://elect.hamiltontn.gov/Portals/12/Archives/2013/2013-03%20Sample.pdf) · [Official result](https://elect.hamiltontn.gov/Portals/12/Archives/2013/0313%20Election%20Summary%20Official.pdf)

The court upheld the right of voters registered in either Hamilton or Marion County to vote if **any part of the lot they lived on** fell within the proposed boundary change. Their house could be outside that area. Someone’s participation in that election therefore cannot place their house inside Chattanooga. [Court’s ruling on eligibility, pages 12–13](https://cases.justia.com/tennessee/court-of-appeals/2014-e2013-00190-coa-r3-cv.pdf#page=12)

## Check a parcel on the county line

1. Find the parcel number and recorded plat or deed reference. A Hamilton assessor card for part of Tract 13, for example, points to **Elder Mountain Subdivision, Plat Book 3, page 80**, at the Marion–Hamilton line. Obtain the recorded plat and the deed for the tract you are checking. [Assessor’s plat reference](https://assessor.hamiltontn.gov/card/134_003.02) · [Hamilton County Register of Deeds](https://www.hamiltontn.gov/RegisterofDeeds.aspx)
2. Give the parcel number to Chattanooga’s Clerk to Council through the city’s public records center. Ask for the signed annexation ordinance, legal description, full map and any later changes covering that parcel. For Tiftonia, identify Ordinance 6397. For an Elder Mountain lot, include the 1994 annexation and the parcel list and map used for Ordinance 12525. The Hamilton County Chancery Court Clerk and Master can also identify trial exhibits. The trial case numbers are **11-0407** for *Little* and **12-0634** for *Rich*. [City records center](https://chattanooga.gov/request-and-report/records/open-records) · [Chancery Court records](https://www.hamiltontn.gov/Courts.aspx)
3. Compare the ordinance description with the recorded parcel and the county line. South of the Tennessee River, section 4 of the 1841–42 Acts, chapter 179, identifies the line marked by the Hiwassee District surveyor general. Ask the register for the applicable survey and any later boundary changes. For taxes or permits, confirm which part of a split lot the office serves. [County-line law](https://www.ctas.tennessee.edu/private-acts/acts-1841-42-chapter-179-0) · [Current county routing and map discrepancy](reach-the-municipal-office.md)
