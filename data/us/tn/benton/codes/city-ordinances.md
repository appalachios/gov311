The mayor and two commissioners make up Benton's city board. An ordinary ordinance needs two readings and at least two affirmative votes. The charter calls for most ordinances to be read aloud by caption. An amendment must set out the affected sections or subsections in full. Ask for the written version before each vote to see what would change. [Benton charter, §§ 2.01, 2.02 and 2.07](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/sites/default/files/private/charters/Benton_cht.pdf)

An ordinary ordinance takes effect 20 days after approval on second reading unless it specifies a later date. Section 2.07 has a narrow exception for emergencies affecting life, health or property. It requires a statement of the emergency facts and limits the ordinance to 90 days. The section also lists subjects that cannot use that route, including taxes. [Benton charter, § 2.07(a)](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/sites/default/files/private/charters/Benton_cht.pdf)

For something pending, ask the city recorder for the next board agenda, proposed text and any amendments. After the vote, ask for the minutes, final ordinance and its effective date. The recorder keeps the originals and the current code. The charter also calls for the official city newspaper to publish a title and short summary within ten days of final approval. [Benton charter, §§ 2.02(c), 2.07(b) and (d)](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/sites/default/files/private/charters/Benton_cht.pdf) · [City recorder](https://polkcountytn.gov/city-offices/)
