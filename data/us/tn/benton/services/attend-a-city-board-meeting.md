For the meeting you want to attend, call the recorder at 423-338-5733, option 3. Ask for the dated notice, location, agenda and proposed ordinances. The charter requires public notice and makes the minutes open for inspection. [Benton charter, § 2.02(c)](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/sites/default/files/private/charters/Benton_cht.pdf) · [City offices](https://polkcountytn.gov/city-offices/)

For a regular meeting, Tennessee law requires the agenda to be available to the public free of charge at least 48 hours beforehand. Ask where it is posted and how to sign up if you want to speak. [Tennessee Comptroller, Open Meetings Act FAQ](https://comptroller.tn.gov/office-functions/open-records-counsel/open-meetings/frequently-asked-questions/tennessee-open-meetings-act-faqs.html)

Polk County and the city's submission to the University of Tennessee both list **10 a.m. on the first Monday** at the Benton Municipal Building. Section 1-101 of the university’s September 2006 code lists **7 p.m.** on the same day. The charter requires the regular time and place to be set by ordinance. Ask for the latest ordinance setting the schedule and use the dated notice to plan your visit. [County schedule](https://polkcountytn.gov/city-offices/) · [Municipal directory](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/directories/cities/benton) · [Published code, § 1-101](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/system/files/codes/combined/Benton-code.pdf)

For **water system decisions**, Benton Water Works’ 2025 water quality report directs customers to a City Commission Board meeting at the utility office at 10 a.m. on the first Thursday. Ask the recorder which meeting will take up your item. [Benton Water Works report, published May 21, 2026, newspaper page 15](https://thepolkcountynews.com/wp-content/uploads/PCN-5-21-26-tearsheets.pdf#page=2)
