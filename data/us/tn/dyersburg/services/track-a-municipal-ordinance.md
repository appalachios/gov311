To follow a proposal in Dyersburg, get the dated meeting notice, agenda, proposed text and attachments. The municipality reports its regular meeting schedule as “1st and 3rd Monday at 7:00 PM at Municipal Building.” Confirm the date and location before attending. [Board agendas and minutes](https://www.dyersburgtn.gov/169/Mayor-Aldermen) · [Meeting schedule and municipal officials](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/directories/cities/dyersburg)

Look up the charter for the governing body’s powers, readings and vote requirements. Compare a proposed ordinance with the current code section it would change. [Municipal Code](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/code/municipal-code-dyersburg) · [Municipal Charter](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/charter/dyersburg)

Under charter § 7, an ordinance needs votes from a majority of the full Board of Mayor and Aldermen. The same section lets the board set its own rules of procedure. Municipal Code § 1-103 adopts Robert’s Rules of Order where consistent with the charter and code. Neither section sets a general reading count. [Charter § 7](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/sites/default/files/private/charters/dyersburg_cht.pdf#page=12) · [Municipal Code § 1-103](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/system/files/codes/dyersburg-t1.pdf#page=2)

One reading can suffice when the charter is silent and no other law or adopted local rule requires more. Dyersburg’s 2025 building and fire code packet lists readings on July 21 and August 4 for those code changes. For another proposal, ask the recorder for any board rule requiring additional readings and the minutes recording final passage. [City code adoption packet](https://www.dyersburgtn.gov/DocumentCenter/View/413/Current-Code-Editions) · [Dyersburg records requests](https://www.dyersburgtn.gov/170/Tennessee-Open-Records-Policy) · [Tennessee Supreme Court decision](https://law.justia.com/cases/tennessee/supreme-court/1968/435-s-w-2d-803-2.html) · [UT guide to ordinance readings](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/reference/procedures-adopting-ordinances)

After the meeting, use the minutes to identify the motion that actually passed. Ask the recorder or clerk for the adopted ordinance, any amendments and the effective date. A proposal can change during consideration, and a recent ordinance may still be waiting to appear in the code. [Tennessee agenda and minutes requirements](https://comptroller.tn.gov/office-functions/open-records-counsel/open-meetings/frequently-asked-questions/tennessee-open-meetings-act-faqs.html)
