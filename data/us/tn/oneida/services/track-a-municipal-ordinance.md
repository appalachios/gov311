The Oneida charter (Private Acts of 1917, chapter 211, as amended) sets no general number of ordinance readings. One reading can suffice under Tennessee law when the charter is silent and no adopted local rule or subject-specific law requires more. Check the rule for the proposal before treating its first vote as final. [Compiled charter](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/sites/default/files/private/charters/ONEIDA_CHT.pdf#page=6) · [Tennessee ordinance procedure](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/reference/procedures-adopting-ordinances) · [Tennessee Supreme Court, *Balsinger*, 435 S.W.2d 803, 805](https://law.justia.com/cases/tennessee/supreme-court/1968/435-s-w-2d-803-2.html)

Under charter § 5, three members constitute a quorum and an ordinance passes with the affirmative vote of a majority of a quorum. Municipal Code § 1-103 adopts Robert’s Rules where consistent with the charter and code. Neither text sets a general reading count. Oneida’s signed 1997 code adoption records two readings. For a pending ordinance, ask the recorder for any later rule on readings and the entry in the ordinance book kept under § 1-302. [Code preface, printed page v](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/system/files/codes/oneida-ip.pdf#page=5) · [Municipal Code §§ 1-103 and 1-302](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/system/files/codes/oneida-t1.pdf) · [Signed code adoption, final page](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/system/files/codes/oneida-cao.pdf#page=4)

To follow a proposal in Oneida, get the dated meeting notice, agenda, proposed text and attachments. The municipality reports its regular meeting schedule as “3rd Thursday at 6:00 PM at Municipal Building.” Confirm the date and location before attending. [Meeting schedule and municipal officials](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/directories/cities/oneida)

For a change to the code, match the proposed chapter or section number to the one in the signed amendment. [Code adoption, § 7](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/system/files/codes/oneida-cao.pdf#page=3)

After the meeting, use the minutes to identify the motion that actually passed. Ask the recorder or clerk for the adopted ordinance, any amendments and the effective date. A proposal can change during consideration, and a recent ordinance may still be waiting to appear in the code. [Tennessee agenda and minutes requirements](https://comptroller.tn.gov/office-functions/open-records-counsel/open-meetings/frequently-asked-questions/tennessee-open-meetings-act-faqs.html)
