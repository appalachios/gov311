To follow a proposal in Byrdstown, get the dated meeting notice, agenda, proposed text and attachments. The municipality reports its regular meeting schedule as “2nd Monday at 5:00 PM at Town Hall.” Confirm the date and location before attending. [Meeting schedule and municipal officials](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/directories/cities/byrdstown)

Under § 2(c) of Byrdstown’s charter, three voting members must be present, and three yes votes are necessary to pass an ordinance. The recorder and three aldermen are the regular voting members. The mayor can break a tie but does not count toward the quorum. The charter sets no general reading count. [Private Acts of 2002, chapter 90](https://capitol.tn.gov/Bills/102/Chapter/PR0090.pdf) · [Compiled charter, § 2](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/sites/default/files/private/charters/byrdstown_cht.pdf#page=4)

One reading can suffice when the charter is silent and no other law or adopted local rule requires more. UT lists no public municipal code for Byrdstown. Call Town Hall at **(931) 864-6215** and ask the recorder for the board’s current rules on ordinance readings and the minutes for the proposal. [Tennessee Supreme Court decision](https://law.justia.com/cases/tennessee/supreme-court/1968/435-s-w-2d-803-2.html) · [UT guide to ordinance readings](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/reference/procedures-adopting-ordinances) · [Town contacts](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/directories/cities/byrdstown)

After the meeting, use the minutes to identify the motion that actually passed. Ask the recorder or clerk for the adopted ordinance, any amendments and the effective date. A proposal can change during consideration, and a recent ordinance may still be waiting to appear in the code. [Tennessee agenda and minutes requirements](https://comptroller.tn.gov/office-functions/open-records-counsel/open-meetings/frequently-asked-questions/tennessee-open-meetings-act-faqs.html)
