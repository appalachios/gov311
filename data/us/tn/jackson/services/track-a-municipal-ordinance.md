Jackson’s council must introduce an ordinance at an open meeting and file it with the city recorder. The council can adopt it only at a later meeting. Look up both agendas and ask the recorder for the text filed at introduction and the version that passed. A resolution can pass at the meeting where the council introduces it. [Jackson charter § 26](https://library.municode.com/tn/jackson/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=CHJATE_S26OR) · [Council agendas and minutes](https://www.jacksontn.gov/government/citycouncil/agendaandminutes)

For publication, use § 26 as amended in 2019. If state law requires newspaper publication, the city must publish a summary in a newspaper of general circulation in Jackson. Otherwise it must publish the ordinance promptly on its official website. An older summary at the end of the published charter still describes newspaper publication for every ordinance. Private Chapter 19, § 4 replaced that language. [Current charter § 26](https://library.municode.com/tn/jackson/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=CHJATE_S26OR) · [2019 amendment and enactment record](https://wapp.capitol.tn.gov/apps/BillInfo/Default?BillNumber=HB1529&GA=111)

If the mayor vetoes an ordinance, the mayor must return it with written reasons before the next regular council meeting. The council can override the veto by a roll-call vote of two-thirds of its entire membership. [Jackson charter § 15](https://library.municode.com/tn/jackson/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=CHJATE_S15COPO)

For a change to an existing ordinance, compare the proposed text with the current code section. Ask the recorder for any amendments passed since that edition. [Municipal code](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/code/municipal-code-jackson)
