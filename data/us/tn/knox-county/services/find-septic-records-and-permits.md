**For an existing system**, use the Knox County Health Department’s file search form. Enter the current owner and the property address and parcel ID as they appear in KGIS. Include your return email and send the form to **kchd.filesearch@knoxcounty.org**. The result can identify a completion or repair approval and the bedroom count it covered. If the county finds no record, that tells you what is on file. It does not establish whether a system is present or functioning. [County file search form](https://www.knoxcounty.org/health/pdfs/EH/SubsurfaceSewageDisposalSystemFileSearchApplication.pdf) · [Groundwater Protection](https://www.knoxcounty.org/health/groundwater_protection/)

**For a new system, remodel or repair**, call Knox County Groundwater Protection at **865-215-5200, extension 1** before starting work. Describe the property and project and ask about the septic permit, drain field layout and inspections. Depending on the work, staff may need a soil map or site plan. The state lists Knox as a contract county with its own septic program. [County permit instructions](https://www.knoxcounty.org/health/groundwater_protection/) · [Tennessee septic contacts](https://www.tn.gov/environment/permits/water/septic-systems-permits/ssp/wr-sds-contacts-by-region.html)

For land near Farragut’s western edge, confirm which county contains the parcel. **Loudon County** directs owners to the Tennessee Department of Environment and Conservation for its septic application and records search. Knox County’s file search is for Knox parcels only. [Loudon County septic permits and records](https://planningandcodes.loudoncounty-tn.gov/buildingCodes_septicSystemContact.php)
