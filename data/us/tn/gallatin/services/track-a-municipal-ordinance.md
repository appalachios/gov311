To follow a proposal in Gallatin, get the dated meeting notice, agenda, proposed text and attachments. The municipality reports its regular meeting schedule as “1st and 3rd Tuesday at 6:00 PM at City Hall.” Confirm the date and location before attending. [Meeting schedule and municipal officials](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/directories/cities/gallatin)

Look up the charter for the governing body’s powers, readings and vote requirements. Compare a proposed ordinance with the current code section it would change. [Municipal Code](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/code/municipal-code-gallatin) · [Municipal Charter](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/charter/gallatin)

Article IV, § 3 of Gallatin’s charter calls for two votes on separate days in open council sessions to pass an ordinance. An ordinance setting the city tax rate needs three votes on separate days. A resolution takes one vote in open session. The city adopted this change on August 15, 1995, by Ordinance 09503-017 after the legislature passed Private Chapter 83. [Gallatin charter, article IV, § 3](https://library.municode.com/tn/gallatin/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=PTICHGATE_ARTIVOR_S3ADPRORRE) · [Charter amendment, § 2](https://capitol.tn.gov/Bills/99/Bill/HB1873.pdf) · [General Assembly passage record](https://wapp.capitol.tn.gov/apps/BillInfo/Default?BillNumber=HB1873&ga=99)

The May 20, 2025 agenda shows how to tell which vote is coming. Item 3 scheduled the third reading of the tax-rate ordinance, O2504-23. Item 6 scheduled the second reading of the annual budget, O2505-29. Look up the ordinance number in the minutes to see whether the council passed it. [City Council agenda and proposed text](https://www.gallatintn.gov/AgendaCenter/ViewFile/Agenda/_05202025-861)

For a rezoning, follow the planning commission recommendation to the Council Committee. Gallatin sends planning actions that require an ordinance through that committee before they reach the City Council agenda. [Charter classification](https://www.mtas.tennessee.edu/charter/gallatin) · [Governing text](https://www.gallatintn.gov/683/Current-Planning)

After the meeting, use the minutes to identify the motion that actually passed. Ask the recorder or clerk for the adopted ordinance, any amendments and the effective date. A proposal can change during consideration, and a recent ordinance may still be waiting to appear in the code. [Tennessee agenda and minutes requirements](https://comptroller.tn.gov/office-functions/open-records-counsel/open-meetings/frequently-asked-questions/tennessee-open-meetings-act-faqs.html)
