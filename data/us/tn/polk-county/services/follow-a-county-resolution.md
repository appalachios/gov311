1. **Before the meeting**, find the date, location and submission deadline on the county’s [meeting notices](https://polkcountytn.gov/meeting-notices/). To follow an item already scheduled, open the PDF for the meeting month under [Agendas & Minutes](https://polkcountytn.gov/agendas-minutes-2/). Check the notice for how to sign up for public comment. Tennessee’s [Open Meetings Act guidance](https://comptroller.tn.gov/office-functions/open-records-counsel/open-meetings/frequently-asked-questions/tennessee-open-meetings-act-faqs.html) explains when agendas must be posted and when the public can speak.
2. **Read the attachment.** An agenda entry gives you the subject. The proposed resolution or agreement contains the amount, property, office or change the commission is considering. If an attachment is missing, ask the County Clerk for the text by meeting date and item number.
3. **After the meeting**, find that date on the minutes themselves. The county often posts the previous meeting’s approved minutes beside a later agenda. Read the motion, any amendment, and the vote. For the rule now in force, ask for the adopted resolution and any later amendment, effective date or executive action. Tennessee law gives the county executive a veto over certain legislative resolutions when the executive does not chair the commission. [Minutes and votes](https://www.ctas.tennessee.edu/eli/minutes) · [Executive action](https://www.ctas.tennessee.edu/eli/relationship-county-legislative-body-county-mayor)

**What that looks like in Polk County.** Item 10 on the August 2026 agenda proposed buying property for the Road Department. Page 3 of the approved minutes records a motion for the county to contribute to the purchase. Four commissioners voted yes and five voted no. That motion failed. The attachment alone would miss the outcome. [August agenda](https://polkcountytn.gov/wp-content/uploads/commission-meetings/agendas/agenda-2026-08.pdf) · [August minutes, page 3](https://polkcountytn.gov/wp-content/uploads/commission-meetings/minutes/minutes-2026-08.pdf#page=3)

For a bill filed in Nashville that names Polk County, use the [private acts guide](../codes/polk-private-acts.md). For a city ordinance, start with the recorder or clerk of the city named in the proposal.
